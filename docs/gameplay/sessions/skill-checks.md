# Skill checks

The game master decides whether an action needs a skill check. A character may succeed without a roll if they often do the action or have a high relevant stat. The game master sometimes asks for a roll during a conversation or a trade with a game master character. A roll can also occur when a character machines a part.

## The roll

The game master chooses the stat side that is closest to the action. For example, the game master can choose Scientific for a skill check to work out how to machine a part. The opposite side of the pair never affects the skill check.

The player rolls a d100 and adds the value of the chosen stat. For example, a roll of 52 and a stat of 15 give a total of 67. A total is from 2 to 200.

Sometimes the game master chooses a target from 1 to 200 before the roll. The action succeeds if the total equals or exceeds the target. At other times, the game master hears the total and then decides whether the action succeeds.

On success, the action goes ahead. On failure, the game master describes the consequences.

## Contests between players

Players can compete, for example with rival bids or sabotage. Each acting player rolls against a target that the game master sets. The game master then rules on the result.

Two actions in the same round can also conflict without a contest. [Sessions and turns](turn-order.md#actions-in-the-same-round) describes that case.
