# Sessions and turns

In a session, the players take turns one at a time. Then the game master takes a turn called the interterm. Today, one round covers one quarter of game time.

## Turn order

The players choose their order at the start of each session. They can agree on an order or use a random order. By habit, the order stays the same for the whole session. A round can continue into the next session.

The group plays only when all players are present.

## Player turns

The group currently allows about 20 minutes of real time for each player. This is the turn limit. This limit can change. A turn holds about 3 major actions. Small talk does not count. Actions are a loose measure, and the game master judges the limit. In earlier sessions, the group limited the number of actions instead of the time.

The turn limit is a soft limit. When time expires, the player can finish their current action. The group sometimes allows extra time. The group sometimes pauses the timer, usually for longer research.

During a turn, a player chooses actions for their character. For example, a character can direct people to build something, speak to someone, or change an order from an earlier turn. The game master plays all other people in the world, responds to actions, and usually introduces events.

Players can also interact with each other during any player's turn. The player whose turn it is usually starts the interaction. The interaction uses the active player's time and counts toward their actions.

Players often record actions and events in their notes. Anyone in the group can research a historical detail, such as an event or the price of an item at the time.

## Standing orders

An order continues every round until the player changes it.

## Actions in the same round

All actions in one round occur in the same quarter of game time. The group treats them as simultaneous. When two actions conflict, the game master resolves them together. The game master decides when to resolve them, case by case. For example, two players try to buy the same oil field. The turn order gives no advantage.

## The interterm

After the last player finishes, the game master takes the interterm. The game master describes events during the quarter and explains their effects. These can include political events, a death, a new product, the founding of a company, or a price increase. The game master decides which real events still happen.

Players can react briefly while the game master describes the events.

The group often saves an event that involves all players for the interterm. For example, the group played the World Fair during the interterm because all three players attended.

## The next round

At the end of the interterm, the group decides whether to play another round. If the group continues, time advances one quarter.

Each payment settles on its own schedule. Monthly income and monthly debt payments count three times in a quarter. A yearly payment counts only in the round in which it falls due. The group also accounts for expenses from the interterm.

## Between sessions

Players can research and plan between sessions. Game actions take place only during sessions.
